1. Introduction

This policy explains what happens to information when you use CamHunt: Hidden Camera Detector (“CamHunt” or “the app”). CamHunt is published under the name DB App Studio (“we,” “us,” or “our”).

CamHunt performs its detection work on your phone. The app also includes Google's advertising, consent and billing services, and those services process information about your device. This policy describes both, separately, so you can see the difference between what we handle and what Google handles.

If you have any questions about this policy, write to camhuntapp.support@gmail.com.

2. Summary

  • Camera images, raw sensor readings and scan results are processed on your phone and are not automatically sent to us. We receive only content that you deliberately include in an email or attachment.
  • We do not operate a server for this app. There is no account, no login and no user database.
  • Unless you subscribe to Pro, the app shows advertisements supplied by Google AdMob. Google's advertising services process device information, including an advertising identifier and your IP address.
  • Subscriptions are sold and processed by Google Play. The app receives limited purchase information from Google Play, described in section 9.
  • We do not sell your personal information, and we do not build advertising profiles ourselves.

We do not claim that the app collects no data, because the Google services inside the app process device information. We do not receive your camera images or scan reports, and CamHunt does not collect or transmit your GPS location. Google's advertising services may estimate an approximate location from your IP address, as explained in section 4.

3. Information processed on your device

Everything in this section stays on your phone unless the text says otherwise.

Camera images

The Lens Reflection, IR Detector, and AI Object Recognition scans read live frames from your camera. Frames are analysed in memory and discarded after processing. CamHunt does not provide a feature for capturing or saving camera photographs or videos, and it does not save or upload camera frames. The AI model used by the AI Object Recognition scan is included inside the app and runs on your phone. No image is sent anywhere for analysis.

Sensor readings

The Magnetometer scan reads your phone's built-in compass sensor to look for magnetic disturbance. Raw magnetometer readings are used during the scan and discarded. A derived score or summary may be retained as part of a saved scan report.

Wi-Fi and network information

The Network scan examines the Wi-Fi network your phone is already connected to. It reads the network name to show you which network is being scanned, determines the local address range, and sends simple connection attempts to other devices on that same local network to see which ones respond like a camera. The app is designed to restrict these probes to local-network addresses and does not follow redirects to external addresses.

Bluetooth information

The Bluetooth scan lists nearby Bluetooth devices that are broadcasting, so you can look for devices you do not recognise. Device names and addresses are shown on screen during the scan and are not uploaded.

Scan reports

When a full scan finishes, CamHunt saves a report automatically to the app's private storage on your phone. A report contains a timestamp, a score, and a short text summary for each detection method. Where the Network scan flagged a single device, that summary includes the private local network address of the device concerned.

You can export a report as a PDF. Exported PDFs are also stored in the app's private storage. A report or PDF leaves your phone only when you choose to share it.

Saving a report image to your gallery

CamHunt does not read your photo library. If you choose to save a report as an image, the app writes that single image into your device gallery, in a CamHunt folder. This happens only when you ask for it, and the app writes the image without reading any other picture on your device.

Settings and app state

The app stores your settings, your theme choice, whether you have completed onboarding, results about your phone's infrared capability, alert preferences, and a record of whether your Pro subscription is active. This is ordinary app data held privately on your device.

Android backup

Saved reports, and certain device-specific and subscription-related preferences, are excluded from Android's cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer. Some other app preferences may still be included in Android backup under the settings you have chosen on your device.

Support messages

CamHunt lets you contact us through the Customer Support, Report a Bug and Suggest a Feature options. These options open your email application with a message addressed to camhuntapp.support@gmail.com.

Depending on the option used, the draft email may contain your message, the email address you enter, images you select, the CamHunt app version, your device manufacturer and model, and your Android version. Your email provider also supplies the sender address used to send the email. Device and app details are included automatically to help us investigate the problem.

Images can be attached only when you select them through Android's photo picker. CamHunt receives access only to the images you choose and cannot browse your gallery.

Email is the only direct communication route built into CamHunt. Nothing is sent to us until you choose to send the email. You can review and change the complete message, including the device details, in your email application before sending it.

5. Permissions

Android describes some permissions in wording that sounds broader than what the app actually does. This section explains the main permissions and access used by CamHunt, why they are used, when runtime permissions are requested, and what happens to the information.

Camera

Why: The camera sensor performs the Lens Reflection, IR Detector, and AI Object Recognition scans.

When: The first time you open one of those scans, never at startup.

What happens: Frames are analysed in memory and discarded. Nothing is saved, uploaded, or shared. Android's permission screen says “take pictures and record video” because that is the standard system wording; CamHunt has no camera photo or video recording feature.

Location (precise and approximate)

Why: On some Android versions and for certain Wi-Fi APIs, Android requires location permission before an app can access Wi-Fi information such as the connected network name. Bluetooth scanning on older Android versions may also require location permission. On Android 13 and newer, some Wi-Fi operations may instead use the Nearby Wi-Fi Devices permission; the current version of CamHunt does not request that permission. CamHunt does not use location permission to determine your physical location.

When: When you start a Wi-Fi or Bluetooth scan that requires it.

What happens: CamHunt does not read your GPS position, store your location, or transmit it. If you decline, the Network scan may continue without displaying the Wi-Fi network name. On older Android versions, Bluetooth scanning may be unavailable without location permission. Separately, Google's advertising services may estimate an approximate location from your IP address, as described in Section 4.

Nearby devices (Bluetooth scan and Bluetooth connect)

Why: To list Bluetooth devices broadcasting nearby and read their names.

When: When you start a Bluetooth scan.

What happens: Results are displayed on screen. The scan is declared to Android with the “never for location” flag, so it is not used to derive your position. Device details are not uploaded.

Wi-Fi state and network state

Why: To know whether you are connected to Wi-Fi, read the network name, and determine the local address range to scan.

When: During the Network scan.

What happens: The information is used during the scan and shown on screen. It is not uploaded.

Internet

Why: The internet is used for two separate purposes. The Network scan contacts other devices on your own local network, while the advertising, consent, and billing components need to reach Google's servers.

When: During a Network scan and while advertising or billing is active.

What happens: CamHunt has no developer-operated server and does not automatically send camera images, scan results, or saved reports to DB App Studio. The Network scan sends only local discovery and connection requests to devices on your current local network.

Vibrate

Why: To provide haptic feedback and scan alerts.

When: When haptic feedback or a supported scan alert occurs.

What happens: The device vibrates briefly. No personal information is involved.

Storage (Android 9 and older only)

Why: To save a report image to the shared device gallery on Android 8 and Android 9.

When: Only when you choose to save a report image. This permission is not requested on Android 10 or newer.

What happens: Only the report image you requested is written to the gallery.

Advertising technologies

The Google Mobile Ads SDK may add Android permissions related to the advertising identifier and Android advertising or attribution services. Google uses these technologies for advertising, measurement, and fraud prevention, as described in Section 4.

Where Android provides controls, you can reset or delete your advertising identifier and manage advertising privacy settings under Privacy or Ads.

Permissions CamHunt does not request

No microphone. No contacts. No SMS or call logs. No permission to post notifications. No background location. No permission to read your photo library.

11. Storage and retention

On your device. Settings and preferences remain in the current installation until you clear the app's data or uninstall the app. Preferences included in Android backup may later be restored by Android under your device and backup settings.

Saved reports are kept as follows:

  • Free users: the app keeps the 3 most recent reports. When a new report is saved, the oldest is deleted automatically, together with its exported PDF.
  • Pro users: reports remain until you delete them, clear the app's data, or uninstall the app.

Temporary module scores older than five minutes are treated as expired and are not reused. They are also cleared when a new full-scan session begins.

With us. We retain support and feedback communications, including attachments, only while reasonably necessary to answer the request and handle related matters. We delete them when they are no longer necessary, unless a longer retention period is required by law or reasonably necessary to establish, exercise or defend legal claims. You may contact us to request their deletion.

With Google. Advertising, consent and billing information is retained by Google under Google's own policies, linked in section 19.

13. Security

Scan data is held in the app's private storage area, which Android protects from other applications on the same device. The app has no user account, so there is no password or login that could be stolen. Connections made by Google's advertising and billing components use encrypted connections.

The Network scan deliberately uses ordinary unencrypted requests when probing devices on your local network because some local devices respond only through those protocols. These requests contain only technical discovery and fingerprinting information. They do not contain camera images, saved reports, support messages, account information or payment information.

No application or service can be completely secure, and we do not claim otherwise.
